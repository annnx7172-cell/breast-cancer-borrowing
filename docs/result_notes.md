Unadjusted efficacy. Among 686 women, 299 (44%) had recurrence or died. Women receiving hormone therapy had longer recurrence-free survival than those who did not (log-rank χ² = 8.6, df = 1, p = 0.003). Median recurrence-free survival was 5.52 years with hormone therapy vs 4.18 years without. The 5-year recurrence-free rate was 58.1% (95% CI 51.4–65.7) vs 43.7% (95% CI 38.2–49.9). Because treated women were older and more often post-menopausal, this comparison is unadjusted; adjusted results follow in the Cox analysis.
Median follow-up was 4.5 years (95% CI 4.3–4.7; reverse Kaplan–Meier). Tumour size differed modestly across grades (one-way ANOVA, F = 3.75 on 2 and 683 df, p = 0.024), explaining about 1% of the variation.
Assumption checks. Schoenfeld residual tests indicated non-proportional hazards for grade, menopausal status, age and hormone receptors (global p = 0.004), but not for treatment (p = 0.63). A Cox model stratified by grade and menopausal status satisfied the assumption (global p = 0.25) and gave a consistent treatment effect (HR 0.70, 95% CI 0.54–0.90).
Logistic regression (secondary). Among 406 women with known 5-year status (280 censored before 5 years were excluded), hormone therapy was associated with lower odds of recurrence or death within 5 years (OR 0.34, 95% CI 0.20–0.56). Because the outcome was common (70%) and incompletely followed patients were excluded, odds ratios overstate the effect; the Cox model is the primary analysis. Variance inflation factors were all below 3, indicating no problematic multicollinearity.
Subgroups. The benefit of hormone therapy was similar in women with 1–3 positive nodes (HR 0.57, 95% CI 0.38–0.86) and 4 or more (HR 0.70, 95% CI 0.51–0.95); interaction p = 0.49. Nodal status was strongly prognostic (HR 2.51 for 4+ vs 1–3 nodes) but did not modify the treatment effect.
The treatment effect was also consistent by menopausal status: pre-menopausal HR 0.69 (95% CI 0.43–1.10) and post-menopausal HR 0.67 (95% CI 0.49–0.90); interaction p = 0.89. The wider interval in pre-menopausal women reflects the small number treated (n = 59).
